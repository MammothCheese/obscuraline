Obscura Line

Blending driving, mechanical puzzles, synth jams and rhythm games, OBSCURA LINE is mysterious railway adventure designed for the unique FM-1 hardware. Journey around a synthwave loop, repair forgotten machines through sound and logic, and uncover the mystery at its heart.

Controls that work everywhere

Most controls are explained in the game as you meet them. These few work everywhere.

PLAYgo on: start, next page, confirm
HOMEback to the map (twice: the title)
SAVE heldskip a puzzle or drive you're stuck on
EDITshow the instruction card again
any knobon the map, choose a station or a line
MASTERvolume

The lit buttons are the ones that do something. Each puzzle's card lists its own controls, and the bar at the bottom of the screen reminds you.

The map

Where you choose stations and lines, and see your grades.

  • Turn any knob to step through the stations you've reached and the lines out of them. PLAY plays a station's puzzles, or drives a line.
  • Solve a station's puzzles to restore it, and the next line opens.
  • FX shows the train's blueprint: the parts you've restored and the button each one uses.

Each restored station opens the next line.

Driving the train

four things to know

Before your first drive, the game shows you the basics on four cards. Here they are, with a little more.

1 · Wake the cab

Every drive starts with the cab's start-up. The screen tells you each step:

  1. PLAY powers on, and the lamps light one by one.
  2. The cab sings a short phrase. Play it back on the keys, the right notes in order.
  3. SEQ sets the reverser forward, then ARP closes the doors.

Missed the phrase? PLAY hears it again. After a couple of wrong tries, the next key blinks.

2 · Power and brake

One knob drives the train: PRESETS.

  • Turn it right for power on five notches.
  • Turn it left for the brake on eight notches. The last is the emergency brake, which costs a point in scoring.
  • In the middle, the train coasts.

Brake early and gently. A train is heavy: it carries on much further than you expect.

3 · Speed limits and the stop

  • The round sign is the speed limit. The small numbers beside it show the next limit and how far away it is.
  • Near the end, the metres count down to the marker. Stop with the front of the train on it: the closer, the better.
  • Once you have the pocket watch, there's a timetable too: arrive on time for more points.

At the platform you get a score for the stop, the time and what happened on the way, graded F to S. Drive any line again to beat it.

4 · The train grows as you go

Puzzles give back parts of the train. Each one brings something to do on the way, and a point for doing it:

REC horna fox or people on the line ahead
SEL callyellow speed boards: point and call
ARP whistleW boards, before tunnels and bridges
FX dimmera train coming the other way

The synth: once the cab's synth is back, the keys play along. In a synth break the line holds your speed while you play on the beat. Later, SEQ changes its voice.

Twelve stations, twelve puzzles

At every station something has stopped working. Each puzzle opens with a card that says what to do and which controls it uses.

  • Most are solved by ear as much as by eye: listen for clicks, beats, throbs and tunes.
  • Some give hints if you take a while.
  • Truly stuck? Hold SAVE to watch the answer play out.

The rides

Two lines are rhythm games instead of drives.

  • You can't fail a ride; how close you are sets your grade.

Something beats beneath it all

Drive the whole loop to find out the mystery.

  • Finish the game to unlock two more modes.
